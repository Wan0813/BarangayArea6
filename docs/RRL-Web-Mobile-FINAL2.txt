CHAPTER 2
REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES

This chapter reviews existing literature and studies that are relevant to the study of Development and Implementation of a Barangay Management Information System for Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6: Resident Complaints, Daily Operations, and Fire Rescue and Response Services. It identifies related works and research gaps that justify the development of the proposed system.

Web and Mobile-Based Management Information Systems for Community Services

Web and mobile-based management information systems have become essential tools for local governments in delivering faster, organized, and transparent community services (Liu, 2022). The following local and foreign studies discuss web and mobile systems for citizen services, complaint handling, and emergency response.

According to Regondola-Baltazar (2025) in her study entitled Evaluation of Public Information System with a case study of Barangay Management Information System in the Philippines. Available at https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5248917. She emphasized objectives, vision, pillars, and best practices of barangay MIS including centralized resident records and public service delivery. While the study provided framework and challenges, it did not develop an actual working web and mobile system with complaint ticketing and rescue dispatch. In relation to the present study, this review serves as baseline justification for developing a complete BMIS for Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6 with resident profiling, complaint logging, daily operations, and report generation.

According to Malajos (2025) in her study on Enhancing Barangay Governance through systematic literature review of best practices and community perceptions of barangay captains in the Philippines. Available at https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5027145. Findings revealed that manual paper-based processes and lack of centralized data remain major barriers to governance efficiency. However, the study was limited to review and perceptions and did not present system implementation. In connection with the present study, this gap is addressed by creating an actual web-based and desktop system with mobile support to replace manual logging in Area 6.

According to Pimentel and Azuelo (2026) in their study on Operational Capabilities in Disaster Emergency Management of Bureau of Fire Protection Personnel in Camarines Norte, Philippines. Available at https://doi.org/10.66050/s34qe772. They assessed BFP personnel capability in disaster emergency response and found need for organized dispatch and coordination tools. The study did not include a digital platform for incident logging. The present study applies this by developing the Fire and Rescue Operations Command with SOS logging, Code Red/Orange/Yellow severity, rescue unit assignment, and evacuation center monitoring.

According to Calicdan and Fabregas (2022) in their study entitled A Web-Based Training and Evaluation System for Government Institutions with Application of Data Analytics. Available at https://doi.org/10.69478/jitc2022v4n2a02. They streamlined government training records and reporting using web technologies and analytics. The system was limited to training institutions and did not cover barangay complaints or emergency services. In relation to the present study, this supports the use of web technologies such as PHP 8.2, Laravel, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and MySQL/MariaDB for centralized barangay database and administrative reports.

According to Pernate and Rosales (2025) in their study entitled BH Niyo App: A Smart Mobile Solution for Boarding House Room Rentals in Ormoc City. Available at https://doi.org/10.69478/best2025v1n2a013. They created a mobile app for community rentals with listing and inquiry features, showing practicality of mobile solutions for local needs. However, the study focused only on rentals without barangay workflow integration. The present study builds on this by adding mobile filing of resident complaints and emergency SOS with photo evidence, status tracking, and admin review for Area 6.

According to Jadhav et al. (2025) who developed Citizen Complaint Redressal and Infrastructure Management System in India. Available at https://doi.org/10.5220/0014604900004852. The system provided ticket-based complaint filing, tracking, and resolution for citizens and infrastructure issues. It lacked integration with rescue dispatch and barangay daily operations. In connection with the present study, this work provides foundation for the Resident Complaints and Grievance Desk with ticket number, priority level, tanod field action, and resolution notes.

According to Kholili and Sulthony (2024) in their study entitled Perancangan Sistem Informasi Desa berbasis Mobile dengan konsep Government to Citizen in Indonesia. INTECH, 5(1). Available at https://doi.org/10.54895/intech.v5i1.2474. They designed a mobile-based village information system using Government-to-Citizen concept for announcements and services. The study did not include desktop admin for comprehensive operations and rescue. The present study extends this by combining mobile, web portal for announcements, and desktop for admin managing residents, complaints, daily operations, and fire rescue.

According to Septiana and Lestari (2025) in their study on Analysis of Fire Emergency Response Readiness at Hospital Building. Journal of Security and Risk Insights, 4(6). Available at https://doi.org/10.55324/josr.v4i6.2561. They evaluated readiness using structured tools and emphasized severity assessment. While the study focused on hospital building, its readiness framework is relevant. The present study adapts this in BDRRMC 24/7 operations center header, active incidents monitoring, sheltered evacuees, and priority census of seniors, PWDs, and infants.

According to Sharma et al. (2026) in their study on CNN-Powered Fire Detection System for Rapid Emergency Response. IEEE. Available at https://doi.org/10.1109/ccic68129.2026.11486226. They applied image classification for fast fire versus non-fire detection to trigger rapid response. The study was technical and device-focused without community management workflow. In relation to the present study, this highlights importance of rapid SOS logging and early warning broadcasts to purok leaders, megaphones, and tanod radios with critical alert directive.

According to Mulyana et al. (2024) in their study on Web-Based Inventory Information System at SMA Negeri 1 Babakancikao Purwakarta, Indonesia. Barometer, 8(2). Available at https://doi.org/10.35261/barometer.v8i2.11150. They managed facility inventory and logistics via web for faster data access and control. The study was limited to school inventory. The present study applies similar web inventory principle to Daily Operations and Logistics including Area 6 multicab, motorcycles, maintenance depot tools, and evacuation relief packs.

Synthesis

The studies mentioned above show that web and mobile-based systems improve community service delivery through centralized data, ticket tracking, and rapid coordination. Local studies by Regondola-Baltazar (2025), Malajos (2025), Pimentel and Azuelo (2026), Calicdan and Fabregas (2022), and Pernate and Rosales (2025) highlight Philippine need to shift from manual paper-based barangay processes to digital platforms, while foreign studies by Jadhav et al. (2025), Kholili and Sulthony (2024), Septiana and Lestari (2025), Sharma et al. (2026), and Mulyana et al. (2024) demonstrate proven designs for complaint redressal, village services, and emergency readiness. However, most focused on single function or review without full integration. The present study addresses this gap by integrating these technologies into one complete and localized web, mobile, and desktop BMIS for Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6, covering resident complaints, daily operations, and fire rescue and response services.
