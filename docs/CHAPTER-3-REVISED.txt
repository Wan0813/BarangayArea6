RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

Research Method

This study will employ a Quantitative Descriptive Research Design, which combines the principles of quantitative and descriptive methods. Quantitative research focuses on collecting and analyzing numerical data (McLeod, 2025), while descriptive research aims to describe the current status or characteristics of a phenomenon as it naturally occurs (Siedlecki, 2020). This approach allows the researchers to determine and describe the management processes of Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6, Rodriguez, Rizal in handling resident records, resident complaints, daily operations, and fire rescue and response services.

Developing a Web, Mobile, and Desktop Application-Based Barangay Management Information System (BMIS) for Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6. The system was developed using PHP 8.2+, TypeScript, TSX, JavaScript, CSS, HTML, and SQL, with React, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS for the frontend, Laravel and PHP for the backend, and MySQL/MariaDB for the database. The data will be gathered from respondents’ feedback through the validated survey questionnaire that is reviewed and approved by the IT validators. This will then be analyzed to describe the system’s effectiveness and identify areas for improvement.

A similar approach was used in the study of Butaslac et al. (2024) titled “A Descriptive Quantitative Study Between Teachers’ Performance and Student Learning Outcomes Among Grade 11 Students.” Their research utilized a quantitative descriptive design with a survey questionnaire and statistical tools such as Mean, Pearson, and Multiple Regression Analysis. The findings of their study demonstrated that this design is effective in describing perceptions and assessing performance based on measurable data. Hence, this supports the appropriateness of using the quantitative descriptive design in evaluating the Barangay Management Information System.

System Design

The project developers used visual representations, or the blueprint of the system architecture, to outline the system’s functions and data management, including Flowcharts (Appendix C, D, and E), Data Flow Diagrams (Appendix F), and Entity Relationship Diagrams (Appendix G). The system also consists of a Website as a resident portal for document requests and community announcements (Appendix N), a Mobile application for filing resident complaints and emergency SOS incidents (Appendix Q), and a Desktop application for barangay officials and administrators managing the overall operation, resident profiling, daily operations, and fire rescue and response documentation (Appendix P).

A flowchart is a diagram that depicts a process, system, or computer algorithm, using shapes and arrows to show the sequence of steps. A Data Flow Diagram (DFD) shows how data moves through a system, from input, through processing, to storage output. An Entity Relationship Diagram is used to model the data structure of a system: entities are the things that are stored in a system, their attributes, and the relationships between them (GeeksforGeeks, 2025).

Project Development

The project developers followed the Agile Methodology, an adaptive and iterative software development approach that promotes flexibility, collaboration, and continuous improvement. This methodology allowed the developers to respond effectively to user feedback and evolving requirements throughout the project. The process involved phases including gathering requirements, design, developing, testing, deployment, and review (GeeksforGeeks, 2025). With the use of this model, the system was designed to be responsive to the needs of the barangay captain, barangay secretary, barangay staff, encoders, and residents, which allowed the developers to work step-by-step while continuously improving the system.

Through this approach, the system was designed to be responsive to the needs of barangay officials and residents, as described in the study’s operational framework. The developers monitored progress at each stage to ensure alignment with the study’s objectives and to meet project deadlines. Through this development, the project developers integrated centralized resident profiling, complaint ticket management with tanod field action, daily operations logging, and fire rescue SOS dispatch with evacuation center monitoring, and also did an early implementation and deployment inside Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6 to ensure that the data that will be collected is accurate before the actual deployment.

The developers designed and developed the proposed system following the Agile Software Development Life Cycle. In each development phase, the gathering of requirements, designing, developing, testing, and implementing were done in close collaboration with the community partner to ensure that the system met their actual operational needs.

After the system was completed, it underwent validation and evaluation. A group of selected IT experts was asked to evaluate the system using an ISO 25010 questionnaire (Appendix I and S). This assessment focused on different software quality characteristics, such as functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, and security, to verify the system’s overall quality and performance. Then, under the supervision of the technical adviser from the Institute of Computing Studies of Colegio de Montalban, the developers prepared the survey questionnaire based on the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Standards (Appendix J). The questionnaire was reviewed and approved by the group of IT validators to ensure its accuracy and relevance to the study. Once the survey instrument was validated, the researchers produced multiple printed copies to provide enough questionnaires for all intended respondents. Before data gathering commenced, a formal letter for deployment was prepared, which was approved by the Capstone Adviser, the Program Head of the Bachelor of Science in Information Technology, the Head of the Office of Student Affairs and Services, the Director of Curriculum, Instruction, Partnership, and Linkages, and the College President of Colegio de Montalban. Only after the approval of all signatories did the researchers proceed with the actual data collection.

Data Gathering Procedure

The data gathering procedure of the study was carried out in several stages to ensure that accurate and reliable data were collected for system development and evaluation. The researchers began by identifying the main problem faced by the community partner, Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6. This was done through an interview with the barangay captain, barangay secretary, and staff to gather essential information about their current manual paper-based operations, challenges, and needed requirements for the proposed system (Appendix R). All necessary details, such as resident profiling and census, complaint tracking and grievance resolution, daily operations and logistics, and fire rescue and response dispatching, were collected to serve as the basis for designing the system’s features. Next, based on the gathered requirements, the researchers proceeded with system design and development.

A letter for Data Utilization (Appendix K) and a Letter for Deployment (Appendix L), together with informing the community partner through messages, were submitted to the management of Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6 to seek permission to deploy the system and distribute the survey questionnaires to the respondents. Upon receiving approval, the project developers personally visited the barangay located in Rodriguez, Rizal to conduct the data collection. The respondents consisted of the barangay captain, barangay secretary, barangay staff, encoders, and selected residents who actively used the system.

During the deployment period, the project developers explained the purpose of the study, the confidentiality of responses, and the proper way to answer the questionnaire. Respondents were also assured that the information gathered would be used strictly for academic purposes. The data collection began on the scheduled date approved by the community partner and lasted until the end of the scheduled date, ensuring all respondents had adequate time to evaluate and provide their feedback about the system (Appendix U). After the retrieval of the accomplished questionnaires, the researchers checked each response for completeness and validity. The gathered data were then encoded, tallied, and analyzed using statistical tools such as the weighted mean to determine the overall perception of the respondents regarding the system’s performance in terms of functionality, usability, reliability, and security. The results were later submitted to the statistician of Colegio de Montalban for review and validation before insertion in the study’s data analysis and interpretation.

Research Instrument

The survey questionnaire was the research instrument used to collect the data from respondents. The survey questionnaire aims to collect clear and organized information from many people, so the researcher can understand and compare their opinions, behaviors, or experiences (Kuphanga, 2024). Through this instrument, the project developers gathered data efficiently and accurately, ensuring the reliability and validity of the responses collected during the data gathering process.

The survey questionnaire was developed by the project developers (Appendix J) and was based on the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Standard used in the IT Experts Validation, which served as the framework for assessing the system’s quality and performance. The questionnaire consisted of a series of statements designed to evaluate the Barangay Management Information System for Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6: Resident Complaints, Daily Operations, and Fire Rescue and Response Services in terms of its purpose, functionality, usability, security, and design.

It also utilized a structured 4-point Likert scale format, with the following options: Strongly Agree (4), Agree (3), Disagree (2), and Strongly Disagree (1). These options represented the level of agreement or satisfaction expressed by each respondent toward each statement regarding the system’s features and overall performance. For validity and reliability, the survey questionnaire was carefully reviewed and validated by the IT validators before distribution to the respondents. Their evaluation ensured that all questions were clear, relevant, and appropriate for measuring the system’s effectiveness from the perspective of barangay officials, staff, and residents. Also, the 4-point Likert scale was used by Nee and Yunus (2020) in their study because it removes the neutral option and encourages respondents to give a clear opinion.

Research Sampling

The study employed a convenience sampling technique. Convenience sampling is a non-probability sampling technique in which respondents are selected based on their availability, accessibility, and willingness to participate in the study (Etikan, Musa, and Alkassim, 2016). This sample method was chosen by the project developers as it enables the researchers to select respondents who are readily available and directly involved in the barangay’s daily operations and services.

Based on coordination with Barangay San Jose Annex Area 6, the barangay is managed by the barangay captain, barangay secretary, and barangay staff including encoders and tanod personnel. These individuals were selected as respondents due to their direct knowledge and involvement in the barangay’s operational processes, resident profiling, complaint resolution, and emergency response activities. Additionally, selected residents who frequently visit the barangay to process concerns, complaints, and requests were selected to represent the community members who often use the barangay’s services, bringing the total number to 30 respondents. The IT experts and educator-validators from Colegio de Montalban were also consulted for technical validation using the ISO/IEC 25010 criteria, separate from the 30 end-user respondents.

Data Analysis

The study utilized a weighted mean and a 4-point Likert scale to analyze the data gathered by project developers. The Weighted Mean is a statistical tool applied to determine the overall perception of the respondents toward each factor of the system, based on their ratings in the Likert scale. The use of the Weighted Mean to interpret Likert-scale data was supported by Leon-Mantero et al. (2020), who stated that it is an effective way to summarize and understand the respondents’ perceptions in a descriptive survey.

The formula used was:

Xw = Σ f(w) / F

Where:
Xw = weighted mean
f = frequency of each response
w = weight of numerical value assigned to each response (4, 3, 2, 1)
fw = sum of the products of each frequency and its corresponding scale value
F = total number of respondents

Scale | Weighted Mean/Equivalent | Verbal Interpretation
4 | 3.26 - 4.00 | Strongly Agree
3 | 2.51 - 3.25 | Agree
2 | 1.76 - 2.50 | Disagree
1 | 1.00 - 1.75 | Strongly Disagree
